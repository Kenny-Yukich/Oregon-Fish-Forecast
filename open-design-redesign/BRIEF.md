# Oregon Fish Forecast — separate Open Design concept

Create an alternate website in its own Open Design project. The existing Oregon Fish Forecast app must remain untouched. This is a comparison design, not a deployment or a replacement.

Confirmed choices: Launch a product; Small multi-page site; Warm human.
Execution choice: Local Codex inside Open Design, explicitly selected by the user.

The product helps Oregon anglers answer four questions: What can I catch? Where can I access the water? How should I fish it? Is it worth going right now?

Let Open Design propose a distinct visual composition, typography, spacing and interaction treatment. Keep the experience simple, approachable and outdoors-oriented, with the water comparison table as the main utility. Existing fish branding and river illustration are available as references; the redesign should feel meaningfully fresh rather than merely re-skinning the existing layout. Avoid a generic marketing landing page, signup forms, testimonials or invented statistics.

Use all 24 records in reference/waters.json. Provide a home/directory view, individual water details, and an approach/sources page. Details can share one template. Search by water, reach, region or species; filter rivers and lakes/reservoirs. Include a count and an empty state.

Keep the eight core table fields: water name, target species, drive time, access, seasonal outlook, current conditions, alerts, and last verified. Default driving origin to Portland, Oregon and keep it editable. Link to Google Maps driving directions using each documented access_point.map_query. Do not invent duration estimates or route to the two unverified gauge locations.

Water details must use the actual species, access, methods, season, report, alerts, source links and review dates in the supplied data. Preserve the named reach, each report's original date, its geographic coverage, and the October 12 source-review date. Show older/undated report context honestly. Readings in this isolated concept are unavailable; do not make up flow, temperature, weather or numerical fishing scores. Label this clearly and subtly in the interface. Known species restrictions remain visible without falsely closing the entire water.

Make the result work at desktop and phone widths, with keyboard navigation, legible contrast and accessible controls. Contain any mobile table scroll. Keep the selected water name visible. Real navigation, filters, expanded details and driving links should work.

Work only in the newly created Open Design project. Do not modify, deploy, start, stop, or reconfigure the original website or its server. Do not touch any other Open Design project. Deliver a browser-viewable artifact with a clear entry file and a concise explanation of the design choices.
